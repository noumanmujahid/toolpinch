Understand your result

What is my actual savings rate?

Calculate your monthly and annual savings rate from take-home income. Future-directed contributions are divided by take-home income. Employer contributions are shown separately because they do not come from take-home pay.

Worked example

Saving $500 and investing $400 from $4,000 take-home pay produces a personal savings rate of 22.5%.

How the calculation works

Future-directed contributions are divided by take-home income. Employer contributions are shown separately because they do not come from take-home pay.

Read our calculation and review methodology.

Should investments count as savings?

Long-term investments can be included as future-directed money, while employer contributions are shown separately from take-home savings.

What should I enter?

Use take-home income and realistic working time whenever possible. The result is an educational estimate and not financial, tax, employment or investment advice.
