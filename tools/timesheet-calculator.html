How to use the timesheet calculator

Use this calculator to total several shifts before submitting a timesheet or checking payroll. Blank rows are ignored, breaks are handled per shift, and overnight work is supported.

Formula

Each shift is converted to minutes, its unpaid break is subtracted, and all completed shifts are added together.

Timesheet rounding: the 7-minute rule

Many employers round clock-in and clock-out times to the nearest quarter hour. Under this practice, 1 to 7 minutes past a quarter hour round down and 8 to 14 minutes round up. US federal rules allow rounding only if it averages out so employees are fully paid for the time they work over time.

Frequently asked questions

How are breaks handled?

Enter the unpaid minutes for each shift. Paid breaks should not be deducted.

Can shifts cross midnight?

Yes. An earlier end time is treated as the next day.

Why show decimal hours?

Many payroll and invoicing systems use decimal hours instead of hours and minutes.

Does this save my timesheet?

No. Calculations stay in the current browser page and are not stored as payroll records.

How do I calculate total hours worked in a week?

Subtract each start time from its end time, remove unpaid breaks, then add the shifts. Convert the total to decimal hours by dividing the minutes by 60.

What is the 7-minute rule?

A quarter-hour rounding practice: 1 to 7 minutes past a quarter hour round down and 8 to 14 minutes round up. It is allowed under federal rules only when it doesn't consistently underpay employees.

How do I write 37 hours 45 minutes on a timesheet?

37.75 decimal hours, because 45 minutes ÷ 60 = 0.75.

Assumptions and limitations

Results depend on the values entered and are educational estimates. Pay figures exclude taxes, deductions and benefits. Workplace rules, rounding practices and legal requirements vary by location and employer.
