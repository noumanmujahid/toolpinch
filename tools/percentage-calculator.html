What Percentage Is One Number of Another?

Solve percentage-of, what-percent and percentage-change questions.

The short answer

How it works

To find X% of Y, multiply Y by X and divide by 100: 20% of 150 = 150 × 20 ÷ 100 = 30. To find what percent X is of Y, divide X by Y and multiply by 100: 45 of 60 = 45 ÷ 60 × 100 = 75%. For percentage change, divide the difference by the starting value: 80 to 100 is (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = +25%.

Worked examples

A pay rise

Your hourly rate goes from $18.00 to $19.80. The change is 1.80 ÷ 18.00 × 100 = 10%.

Test score

You scored 42 out of 48. 42 ÷ 48 × 100 = 87.5%.

Price drop

A $250 phone falls to $200. (200 − 250) ÷ 250 × 100 = −20%.

Reverse a markup

A price of $138 includes a 15% markup. 138 ÷ 1.15 = $120 before the markup.

Common mistakes

  • Up and down are not symmetric. A 20% fall followed by a 20% rise does not return to the start: 100 → 80 → 96. Going from 100 to 80 is −20%, but going from 80 back to 100 is +25%.
  • Percent versus percentage points. An interest rate rising from 4% to 5% is up 1 percentage point, but 25% in relative terms.
  • Dividing by the wrong base. Percentage change always divides by the starting value, not the new one.
  • Adding percentages of different totals. 10% of one group and 10% of another is not 20% of the combined group unless the groups are the same size.

Mental-math shortcuts

10% is the number divided by 10; 5% is half of that; 1% is the number divided by 100. 25% is a quarter, 50% a half, 75% three quarters. So 15% of $60 is $6 + $3 = $9, and 35% of 80 is 8 + 8 + 8 + 4 = 28. X% of Y is also the same as Y% of X: 8% of 50 is the same as 50% of 8, which is 4.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

Multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100. For example, 20% of 150 is 150 × 20 ÷ 100 = 30.

How do I work out what percent one number is of another?

Divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100. 45 out of 60 is 45 ÷ 60 × 100 = 75%.

How do I calculate percentage increase or decrease?

Subtract the old value from the new value, divide by the old value and multiply by 100. A positive result is an increase and a negative result is a decrease.

How do I find the original number before a percentage was added?

Divide by 1 plus the percentage as a decimal. If $138 includes a 15% markup, the original was 138 ÷ 1.15 = $120.

What is the difference between percent and percentage points?

Percentage points measure the simple difference between two percentages (4% to 5% is 1 point). Percent change measures the relative change (4% to 5% is a 25% increase).

Why doesn't a 50% loss and a 50% gain cancel out?

Because each percentage is taken from a different base. 100 falls 50% to 50, and a 50% gain on 50 only brings it back to 75.
