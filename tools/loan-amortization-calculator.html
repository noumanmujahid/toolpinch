The short answer

How it works

A fixed-rate loan payment is P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is the amount borrowed, r is the interest rate per payment and n is the number of payments. A $20,000 loan at 7% over 5 years costs $396.02 a month and $3,761.44 in total interest.

The 15-year mortgage costs $717 a month more than the 30-year one but saves over $212,000 in interest.

How amortization works

Each payment first covers the interest due on the current balance; the rest reduces the balance. Early payments are mostly interest. On the $300,000 mortgage at 6.5%, the first month's interest is 300,000 × 0.065 ÷ 12 = $1,625, so only $271.20 of the $1,896.20 payment reduces the balance. As the balance falls, the interest share shrinks and the principal share grows, which is why the schedule pays the loan down slowly at first and faster near the end.

Ways to pay less interest

  • Shorter term: higher payments, much less total interest.
  • Extra principal payments: every extra amount reduces the balance that interest is charged on. Check that your lender has no prepayment penalty.
  • Biweekly payments: paying half the monthly amount every two weeks makes 26 half-payments, or 13 full payments a year, which shortens a 30-year mortgage by several years.
  • Lower rate: on a $300,000, 30-year loan, each 1 percentage point of rate changes the payment by roughly $200 a month.

What the calculator does not include

The payment covers principal and interest only. Mortgage payments often also include property tax, homeowners insurance and mortgage insurance held in escrow. Fees, variable rates and interest-only periods are not modelled.

Frequently asked questions

How is a loan payment calculated?

With the amortization formula P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r the rate per period (annual rate ÷ payments per year) and n the number of payments.

What is the monthly payment on a $300,000 mortgage at 6.5%?

$1,896.20 a month over 30 years, or $2,613.32 over 15 years, for principal and interest only.

Why is most of my early payment interest?

Interest is charged on the outstanding balance, which is highest at the start. As you pay the balance down, less of each payment goes to interest.

Do biweekly payments save money?

Yes, if the lender applies them as they arrive. 26 half-payments a year equal 13 monthly payments, so you pay down principal faster and pay less interest.

What is an amortization schedule?

A table showing every payment over the life of the loan, split into interest and principal, with the remaining balance after each payment.

Does this include taxes and insurance?

No. It shows principal and interest only. Add escrow items such as property tax and insurance separately.
