The short answer

How it works

Multiply each course's grade points by its credits, add the results and divide by the total credits. An A (4.0) in a 3-credit course, a B (3.0) in a 4-credit course, an A− (3.7) in 3 credits and a C (2.0) in 2 credits give 39.1 quality points ÷ 12 credits = 3.26 GPA.

Cumulative GPA and raising your GPA

To combine terms, add all quality points and divide by all credits. With a 3.20 GPA over 60 credits (192 quality points) and this term's 39.1 over 12 credits, the new cumulative GPA is (192 + 39.1) ÷ 72 = 3.21. The more credits you have completed, the more slowly your cumulative GPA moves, so improvements show fastest early on and in high-credit courses.

Weighted versus unweighted: many US high schools add 0.5 or 1.0 point for honors, AP or IB classes (so an A can count as 5.0). Colleges often recalculate on an unweighted 4.0 scale.

Frequently asked questions

How is GPA calculated?

Multiply each course's grade points by its credit hours to get quality points, add them up and divide by total credit hours.

What is a 3.5 GPA as a letter grade?

Roughly between a B+ (3.3) and an A− (3.7) average on a 4.0 scale.

How do I calculate cumulative GPA?

Add the quality points from all terms and divide by all credits attempted. Do not simply average the term GPAs unless every term had the same number of credits.

What is the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA?

Unweighted GPA uses the 0–4.0 scale for every class. Weighted GPA adds extra points for harder classes such as honors, AP or IB, so it can exceed 4.0.

Do pass/fail courses count toward GPA?

Usually not. Pass/fail credits count toward graduation but carry no grade points at most schools; check your school's policy.

How can I raise my GPA quickly?

Focus on high-credit courses, since they carry the most weight, and on retaking failed courses if your school replaces the old grade.
