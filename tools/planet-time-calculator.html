How to use the planet time converter

Compare an Earth duration with one selected planet using approximate solar-day lengths and orbital periods.

Formula or method

Earth time is converted to Earth days, then divided by the selected planet’s solar day and orbital period.

Worked examples

One Earth year on Mars

365.2425 Earth days is about 0.53 of a Mars orbit because a Mars year lasts about 687 Earth days.

One Earth day on Jupiter

Twenty-four Earth hours covers more than two Jupiter solar days because Jupiter rotates in roughly 9.9 hours.

A week on Mercury

Seven Earth days is only a small fraction of Mercury’s approximately 176-Earth-day solar day.

Frequently asked questions

What does a planetary day mean here?

It means an approximate solar day, measured from one local noon to the next, rather than a sidereal rotation.

What does a planetary year mean?

It is one orbit around the Sun, expressed in Earth days.

Why is Venus unusual?

Venus rotates very slowly and in the opposite direction to most planets, so its solar day is exceptionally long.

Are these exact mission-planning values?

No. They are rounded educational comparisons and should not be used for navigation or scientific operations.

Limitations

Values are educational approximations and do not describe biological aging.
