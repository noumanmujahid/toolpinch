The short answer

How it works

The accounting equation is Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Rearranged, Equity = Assets − Liabilities and Liabilities = Assets − Equity. A business with $50,000 of assets and $20,000 of liabilities has $30,000 of equity.

What each part means

  • Assets: what the business owns or controls: cash, money owed by customers (receivables), inventory, equipment, property.
  • Liabilities: what it owes to others: loans, supplier bills (payables), wages owed, taxes due.
  • Equity: the owners' claim on what is left: money the owners put in plus profits kept in the business, minus losses and withdrawals.

How transactions keep it balanced

After these six transactions: assets $16,200 = liabilities $10,000 + equity $6,200. Every transaction changes at least two items so that both sides stay equal; this is the basis of double-entry bookkeeping.

The expanded equation

Because profit increases equity, the equation is often written as Assets = Liabilities + Owner's capital + Revenue − Expenses − Drawings. This shows how day-to-day income and spending flow into the balance sheet.

Negative equity means liabilities are larger than assets: the business owes more than it owns. It is a warning sign for lenders, though new businesses with start-up losses can show it for a while.

Frequently asked questions

What is the accounting equation?

Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Everything a business owns is financed either by borrowing (liabilities) or by its owners (equity).

How do I calculate equity?

Subtract total liabilities from total assets. $50,000 of assets minus $20,000 of liabilities is $30,000 of equity.

Why must the accounting equation always balance?

Because every transaction is recorded in at least two places in double-entry bookkeeping. If one side changes, another item changes by the same amount.

What is the expanded accounting equation?

Assets = Liabilities + Owner's capital + Revenue − Expenses − Drawings. It splits equity into the parts that change it.

What does negative equity mean?

Liabilities exceed assets, so the business owes more than it owns. Equity is negative when accumulated losses and withdrawals are larger than the owners' investment.

Is the accounting equation the same as a balance sheet?

The balance sheet is the accounting equation in detail: it lists assets on one side and liabilities and equity on the other at a point in time.
