What Return Did Your Investment Produce?

The short answer

How it works

ROI = net gain ÷ total cost × 100. If you invest $1,000, pay $50 in costs and end with $1,250, the net gain is $200 and ROI is 200 ÷ 1,050 × 100 = 19.05%. The investment multiple is final value ÷ total cost: 1,250 ÷ 1,050 = 1.19×.

Worked examples

Shares

Bought for $2,000, sold for $2,600, $20 in fees. Net gain $580. ROI = 580 ÷ 2,020 = 28.7%.

A loss

Invested $2,000, now worth $1,700. Net loss $300. ROI = −15%.

Marketing campaign

Spent $500 on ads that produced $1,500 of gross profit. Net gain $1,000. ROI = 200%. Use profit, not revenue.

Equipment

A $3,000 machine saves $1,200 a year. After 3 years: (3,600 − 3,000) ÷ 3,000 = 20%. Payback takes 2.5 years.

Annualized ROI: comparing different time periods

Plain ROI ignores time. A 50% return over 3 years is not the same as 50% in one year. To compare, convert to an annual rate: annualized ROI = (1 + ROI)1 ÷ years − 1. For 50% over 3 years: 1.51/3 − 1 = 14.5% a year.

What ROI leaves out

  • Time: use the annualized figure to compare investments held for different periods.
  • Risk: two investments with the same ROI can carry very different chances of loss.
  • Cash flows along the way: dividends, rent or extra contributions need to be included in the gain or cost. For many irregular cash flows, IRR is the better measure.
  • Inflation and tax: a 5% return with 3% inflation is about a 2% real return, before any tax on the gain.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate ROI?

Subtract the total cost from the final value to get the net gain, divide by the total cost and multiply by 100. $1,250 back on $1,050 of total cost is a 19.05% ROI.

What is a good ROI?

It depends on the risk and time involved. Broad stock market indexes have historically returned roughly 7% to 10% a year before inflation over long periods, so many people use that as a benchmark for risky investments.

How do I calculate annualized ROI?

Use (1 + ROI) raised to the power of 1 ÷ years, minus 1. A 50% total return over 3 years is about 14.5% a year.

Should ROI use revenue or profit?

Profit. For marketing or business projects, use the gross profit the spending produced, not total sales, or the ROI will be overstated.

Can ROI be negative?

Yes. If the final value is less than the total cost, the net gain is negative and so is the ROI.

What is the difference between ROI and the investment multiple?

ROI is the gain as a percentage of cost. The multiple is final value ÷ cost. A 1.5× multiple equals a 50% ROI.
