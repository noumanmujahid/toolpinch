The short answer

How much food and water should you store for an emergency?

Plan on 1 gallon (about 3.8 litres) of water per person per day (US Ready.gov) and about 2,200 calories of food per adult per day (German government guidance). For a family of four, 10 days means about 40 gallons (150 litres) of water and 88,000 calories of food that keeps without a fridge.

Most governments ask for at least 72 hours of supplies. Sweden and Norway suggest one week, and Germany up to 10 days. Use the calculator above for your exact household.

Why are 33 countries telling people to stock up?

In late October 2026, a map of at least 33 countries that advise households to keep emergency supplies spread widely online. The advice itself is not new and is not a warning about a specific event. Governments update it as they plan for power cuts, extreme weather, cyberattacks and supply disruptions. The EU's Preparedness Union Strategy, adopted in March 2025, asks every household to be able to manage on its own for at least 72 hours.

Germany's 10-day food list (per person)

The German Federal Office of Civil Protection publishes this example for one adult for 10 days, about 2,200 calories a day, plus 20 litres of drinks:

Food groupAmount for 10 days
Cereals, bread, potatoes, pasta, rice3.5 kg (7.7 lb)
Vegetables and pulses (canned or jarred)4 kg (8.8 lb)
Fruit and nuts2.5 kg (5.5 lb)
Milk and milk products (UHT milk, hard cheese)2.6 kg (5.7 lb)
Fish, meat, eggs or egg powder1.5 kg (3.3 lb)
Fats and oils0.357 kg (0.8 lb)

It is an example, not a rule. Swap in foods your household already eats, and rotate them so nothing expires.

Tips that make a supply actually useful

Build it slowly

Buy one or two extra items with each weekly shop instead of everything at once. It costs less and avoids empty shelves for others.

Rotate what you store

Use the oldest items in normal cooking and replace them. Store-bought bottled water keeps until its best-before date; replace tap water you bottle yourself about every 6 months.

Plan for no power

Choose food that needs little or no cooking, keep a manual can opener, and have light, a radio and a charged power bank ready.

Frequently asked questions

How much water do I need for an emergency?

Ready.gov recommends at least 1 gallon (about 3.8 litres) per person per day, for drinking and basic hygiene. Germany's guidance is 2 litres per person per day for drinking and cooking. For 2 people for 10 days that is 20 gallons at the US rate.

How many days of food and water should I store?

At least 72 hours is the minimum in the EU, UK, France, Canada and Japan. Sweden and Norway suggest about a week, Germany up to 10 days, and many US states recommend being ready for 2 weeks.

Why are 33 countries telling people to stockpile food?

Governments are updating routine preparedness advice for power cuts, extreme weather, cyberattacks and supply disruptions. A map of at least 33 countries giving this advice went viral in October 2026. The advice is not tied to a specific announced emergency.

How much food does one person need for 10 days?

About 22,000 calories, or 2,200 a day. Germany's example list for one adult is 3.5 kg of grains, bread, pasta or rice, 4 kg of vegetables and pulses, 2.5 kg of fruit and nuts, 2.6 kg of milk products, 1.5 kg of fish, meat or eggs and about 0.36 kg of fats and oils.

What should be in a basic emergency kit?

Water, food that keeps without a fridge, prescription medicines, a first aid kit, a flashlight, a battery or hand-crank radio, spare batteries, a charged power bank, some cash, copies of important documents, a manual can opener and hygiene items.

How long does stored water last?

Commercially bottled water keeps until its best-before date if unopened and stored somewhere cool and dark. Ready.gov suggests replacing water you store yourself about every six months.

Do I need to buy everything at once?

No. Adding a few long-lasting items to each normal shop builds a supply within a few weeks, costs less and avoids panic buying.
