The short answer

How it works

The average (arithmetic mean) is the sum of the values divided by how many there are. For 4, 8, 15, 16, 23 and 42 the sum is 108, and 108 ÷ 6 = 18.

Worked examples

Weekly hours

Worked 38, 42, 40, 36 and 44 hours: 200 ÷ 5 = 40 hours a week on average.

Test scores

Scores of 72, 85, 90 and 81: 328 ÷ 4 = 82.

Monthly spending

$1,240 + $1,180 + $1,420 = $3,840 over 3 months, an average of $1,280.

When the plain average misleads

  • Outliers. Salaries of $40k, $45k, $50k, $55k and $300k average $98k, but four of the five people earn far less. The median ($50k) describes the typical value better.
  • Averaging averages. A class of 20 averaging 70% and a class of 30 averaging 80% have a combined average of (20 × 70 + 30 × 80) ÷ 50 = 76%, not 75%. Weight each average by its group size.
  • Rates and speeds. Driving 60 km at 30 km/h and 60 km back at 60 km/h averages 40 km/h, not 45, because you spend longer at the slower speed. Divide total distance by total time.
  • Percentage returns. +50% one year and −50% the next averages 0% arithmetically, but $100 becomes $75. Use the geometric mean for growth rates.

Mean, median and mode

The mean is the average above. The median is the middle value when sorted. The mode is the value that appears most often. For symmetric data the three are close; for skewed data such as incomes or house prices the median is usually the better summary. The mean, median and mode calculator gives all three plus the range.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate an average?

Add all the values and divide by how many values there are. The average of 4, 8, 15, 16, 23 and 42 is 108 ÷ 6 = 18.

What is the difference between average and median?

The average (mean) uses every value's size; the median is the middle value. A few very large or small values pull the mean but barely move the median.

How do I calculate a weighted average?

Multiply each value by its weight, add the results and divide by the sum of the weights.

Can I average percentages?

Only if they are based on groups of the same size. Otherwise weight each percentage by its group size, or go back to the underlying totals.

How do I calculate average speed?

Divide total distance by total time. Averaging the speeds of each leg gives the wrong answer when the legs take different amounts of time.
