The short answer

How much tax do you pay in Canada in 2026?

Canadians pay federal tax (14% to 33%), provincial or territorial tax (4% to 25.75%), CPP (5.95% of earnings between $3,500 and $74,600, plus 4% up to $85,000) and EI (1.63% up to $68,900). On a $75,000 salary in Ontario you take home about $56,926 a year, or $4,744 a month.

2026 is the first full year at the new 14% lowest federal rate, cut from 15% on July 1, 2025.

How the calculator works

Taxable income is salary minus RRSP contributions and the deductible enhanced part of CPP/QPP (the 1% first additional contribution and all of CPP2). Federal and provincial tax are applied to taxable income, then reduced by non-refundable credits for the basic personal amount, base CPP, EI and (federally) the $1,501 Canada employment amount. Not included: other credits and deductions (spousal, dependants, tuition, medical, union dues), the Quebec deduction for workers, low-income tax reductions in some provinces, and benefits such as the GST/HST credit or Canada Child Benefit.

Frequently asked questions

How much is $75,000 after tax in Ontario?

About $56,926 a year or $4,744 a month in 2026, after $8,259 federal tax, $4,446 Ontario tax and health premium, and $5,370 CPP and EI.

How much is $50,000 after tax in Ontario?

About $40,145 a year or $3,345 a month.

How much is $100,000 after tax in Ontario?

About $74,206 a year or $6,184 a month.

What are the 2026 federal tax brackets in Canada?

14% up to $58,523, 20.5% to $117,045, 26% to $181,440, 29% to $258,482 and 33% above.

What is the basic personal amount for 2026?

$16,452 federally for most people, reducing to $14,829 at higher incomes. Each province has its own amount, from $11,188 in Newfoundland and Labrador to $22,769 in Alberta.

What are the CPP and EI rates for 2026?

CPP is 5.95% of earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 (maximum $4,230.45), plus CPP2 at 4% up to $85,000 (maximum $416). EI is 1.63% of earnings up to $68,900 (maximum $1,123.07).

Which province has the lowest income tax?

For most incomes, Alberta, Nunavut and the Northwest Territories have the lowest combined tax, while Quebec and the Atlantic provinces are generally highest.

Does an RRSP contribution reduce my tax?

Yes. RRSP contributions are deducted from taxable income, so they save tax at your marginal rate. They do not reduce CPP or EI.
