The short answer

How does overtime work in Washington?

In Washington State, non-exempt employees earn 1.5× their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. From January 1, 2027, salaried employees of large employers must earn at least $92,196 a year to be exempt from overtime.

In 2027 the exempt salary minimum for large employers rises to 2.5 times the minimum wage, while smaller employers stay at 2.25 times. Salaried workers below these levels must generally be paid overtime, even if their duties would otherwise be exempt.

Worked example

At $24 an hour, a 46-hour week pays 40 × $24 = $960 regular plus 6 × $36 = $216 overtime, a total of $1,176 before taxes.

Does the "no tax on overtime" deduction apply?

Starting with 2025 tax returns, workers can deduct qualified overtime pay on their federal income tax return, up to $12,500 a year ($25,000 for married couples filing jointly). The deduction phases out above $150,000 of modified adjusted gross income ($300,000 joint). Only the extra part of overtime pay, the "half" in time-and-a-half, counts, and only for overtime the federal Fair Labor Standards Act requires: hours over 40 in a workweek. Social Security and Medicare are still withheld from overtime pay. The calculator above estimates the federal premium for your week.

Frequently asked questions

When does overtime start in Washington State?

After 40 hours in a workweek for non-exempt employees, at 1.5x the regular rate.

What is the Washington exempt salary threshold for 2027?

$92,196 a year ($1,773 a week) for employers with 51 or more employees, and $82,976.40 a year ($1,595.70 a week) for employers with up to 50 employees.

What is the Washington minimum wage in 2027?

$17.73 an hour from January 1, 2027, up from $17.13 in 2026. Some cities set higher local minimums.

Does Washington have daily overtime?

No. Overtime is based on hours over 40 in a workweek.
