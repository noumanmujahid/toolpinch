How Much of Your Salary Should Go to Expenses?

A practical explanation with a clear formula, realistic example and a calculator you can use privately.

By ToolPinch Editorial · Reviewed September 29, 2026 · 5 minute read

Begin with committed expenses

Add housing, utilities, food, transport, insurance, minimum debt payments and other unavoidable monthly commitments.

Calculate the expense-to-income ratio

Divide committed monthly expenses by monthly take-home pay and multiply by 100. Expenses of $2,650 against $4,000 income equal 66.3%.

There is no universal ideal percentage

Housing markets, family size, healthcare costs and debt obligations vary. The ratio is most useful for tracking your own flexibility over time.

Protect room for irregular costs

A budget that allocates every dollar to recurring bills may fail when annual fees, repairs or medical costs appear. Leave a realistic margin.

Try the calculation yourself

Divide take-home pay across needs, wants, debt, savings and investing.
