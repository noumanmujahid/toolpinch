What Is My Time Really Worth?

A practical explanation with a clear formula, realistic example and a calculator you can use privately.

By ToolPinch Editorial · Reviewed September 29, 2026 · 5 minute read

Start with the money you can use

Take-home pay is usually the clearest starting point for a personal decision because it reflects what reaches your account after payroll deductions.

Count the time work actually takes

Paid hours alone can understate the commitment. Include unpaid preparation or commuting when you want a fuller view of the time exchanged for income.

Calculate an effective hourly wage

Divide annual take-home pay by the number of workdays in a year, then divide by the hours connected to each workday.

Use time value without judging every purchase

A low number of work hours does not automatically make something worthwhile, and a high number does not make it wasteful. The result creates perspective.
