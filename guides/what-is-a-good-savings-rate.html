What Is a Good Savings Rate?

A practical explanation with a clear formula, realistic example and a calculator you can use privately.

By ToolPinch Editorial · Reviewed September 29, 2026 · 5 minute read

Define what counts as saving

Cash reserves, retirement contributions and long-term investments can count when the money is genuinely directed toward your future rather than a near-term purchase.

Savings-rate formula

Divide personal monthly savings and investments by monthly take-home income, then multiply by 100. Track employer contributions separately for clarity.

Example savings rate

Saving $500 and investing $400 from $4,000 take-home pay produces a 22.5% personal savings rate before employer contributions.

A sustainable rate is more useful than a perfect rate

Income stability, debt and essential expenses affect what is realistic. Consistency and gradual improvement are often more informative than comparison with one benchmark.
